# Code of conduct

Vibld is built in the open by people who disagree about code, often and usefully. This page is about how we treat each other while we do it. It applies in this repository's issues, pull requests, discussions and reviews, and anywhere else someone is speaking for the project.

## What we ask

- **Argue with the idea, not the person.** Say what is wrong with a change and why. Leave out anything about the person who wrote it.
- **Be specific.** "This breaks the export on Windows paths, here is the input" helps. "This is sloppy" does not.
- **Assume the other person has context you lack**, and ask for it before concluding they got it wrong.
- **Credit work.** Say where an idea or a piece of code came from, including when it came from someone who disagrees with you.
- **Make room.** Newcomers, people writing in a second language and people who are new to a part of the code all get the same patience you would want.
- **Take "no" for an answer.** A maintainer can decline a change. Ask why, once, and then accept the answer or take it to a discussion.

## What is not acceptable

- Harassment of any kind, in public or in private messages, including unwelcome sexual attention and deliberate intimidation.
- Insults, slurs and comments about someone's identity, body, background or ability.
- Publishing someone's private information, such as an address or an employer, without their permission.
- Repeatedly pushing a point after being asked to stop, or following someone across threads to continue an argument.
- Using the project's spaces to advertise, or to promote anything that harms people.

## Reporting

Email **hello@vibld.com**. Say what happened, where (links help), and whether you want to stay anonymous to the other person. A maintainer will read it, reply to acknowledge it, and keep who reported it private.

If the problem is a security vulnerability rather than behaviour, use the private route in [SECURITY.md](SECURITY.md) instead.

## What happens next

The maintainers decide what to do, in proportion to what happened and whether it has happened before. In rising order, that can be:

1. A private word, saying what the problem was and what to do instead.
2. A written warning, and for a time no contact with the people involved.
3. A temporary ban from the project's spaces.
4. A permanent ban.

Anything posted that breaks this code may be edited, hidden or removed. If you are the subject of a decision and think it was wrong, reply to the maintainer who told you, and a different maintainer will look at it where there is one.

Maintainers are held to this code too. A report about a maintainer is decided by the other maintainers, without the one concerned. While the project has a single maintainer (see [GOVERNANCE.md](GOVERNANCE.md)) that is not yet possible, and you should know that before you write.
